On an ordinary day

One thing at a timedoesn't hold me.

If I'm watching TV, I'm also making something, which is how I ended up with so many crafts. A trip to a supermarket across town needs a second errand, or it feels wasted.

I leave with one plan and often come back having done something else.

How to talk to me

Be direct.Tell me everythingat once.

Text or in person both work. If something isn't working, tell me exactly what, and what you would do instead.

One thing I'll ask of you

Easy to work with.Boundaries set early.

What bothers me is being asked not to do something and then seeing it happen again, or being contacted at times I've said I'm off.

Before I say yes

Evidence first.Then I come roundto it too.

I read, compare and check before I choose. By the time I've decided, I usually believe in it.

If we work together

A clear goal,a part I own,regular check-ins.

Working alone too long, I lose perspective, so I like to meet regularly and trade feedback.

My favorite moments: coming up with the idea, and making the thing.

What I can't leave alone

If something is clumsy,I rebuild it.

My Kindle sat unused because it couldn't open the web novels I actually read, so I worked out how to make it. My TV box took five to eight clicks to open an app; with AI's help I rebuilt its home screen.

Lately, with AI, I feel I can make far more than I could before.

Where to find me on a weekend

Every item I buyhas its ownsupermarket.

Supermarkets are my favorite places to explore, especially a chain I haven't tried yet. Pricing, product range and the interior always say a lot about each other, so I compare them for fun.

It has made my shopping oddly specific: every item has the one store I buy it from, so some days I visit several.

In a new city

I learn a cityon foot.

In New York I'd plan a quick route, head out, and end up wandering with no route at all; I always found something new. In the pocket gardens squeezed between towers, I kept thinking there must be so many stories happening there.

In a new city I look up what's on that weekend; in Shanghai that meant a lot of hands-on workshops. In Philadelphia I head out of town. In Vancouver it was a library downtown, for the atmosphere.

What my hands are busy with

If it can be madeby hand, I'veprobably tried.

A crochet bag, a wooden folding chair as a kid, press-on nails, ceramics, a lot of jigsaws and Lego. And the furniture layout of my own home, which I'm still pleased with.

One thing people get wrong

I look serious.I'm actuallyvery funny.

Most friends say I turned out much more fun than they expected.
